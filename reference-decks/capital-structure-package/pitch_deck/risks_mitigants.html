Underwriting discipline
Key risks and mitigants
Separating the entities improves capital efficiency, but it does not eliminate execution, financing, governance, or demand risk.
Risk to underwrite
Illustrative mitigation
01 / TECHNOLOGYDevelopment and integration
Use milestone-based budgets, staged hiring, interface specifications, commissioning plans, acceptance tests, and documented change-order controls.
02 / FACILITYCompletion and cost overruns
Define responsibility for permits, construction delays, defects, contingencies, completion readiness, and any required completion or funding support.
03 / EQUIPMENTUtilization before ramp
Stage purchases against validated demand, negotiate installation or payment holidays where available, maintain minimum liquidity, and match amortization to useful life.
04 / IPLeakage into the JV
Attach a detailed IP schedule, use limited operating licenses, require invention assignment, and define ownership of custom improvements before work begins.
05 / GOVERNANCEDeadlock across entities
Set reserved matters, approval thresholds, escalation procedures, capital-call mechanics, default-funding remedies, and buy-sell or step-in rights.
06 / DEMANDRevenue below plan
Pursue anchor customers, stage capacity, align equipment orders with contracted or validated demand, and consider deposits or take-or-pay structures where commercially feasible.
Investor takeaway: the structure makes each risk visible, assignable, and financeable rather than hiding all of it inside one equity round.
Illustrative discussion material only; mitigants are proposed controls, not guarantees of performance or financing availability.
